GREEN TEA

Matcha (抹茶)

An introduction to matcha: its origins, the ritual of preparation, and the science behind the cup.

01 / THE ORIGINS

From China to Japan.

Matcha’s roots trace back to China, where powdered tea was mixed with hot water and whisked during the Song dynasty (960–1279). In 1191, the Buddhist monk Eisai returned from China with tea seeds and helped introduce this method of preparing tea to Japan.

Over the following centuries, Japan developed its own growing and processing techniques, shaping the matcha we know today. Matcha also became central to the Japanese tea ceremony, turning the preparation and sharing of tea into a practice of care and attention.

02 / MORE THAN A GREEN CUP

Health Benefits.

Matcha is a form of green tea, with caffeine and plant compounds that researchers study for their potential benefits. Much of the evidence comes from green tea or extracts, rather than matcha itself.

03 / THE LITTLE RITUAL

Make your first cup.

You’ll need matcha, warm water, a small sieve, a bowl, and a bamboo whisk or handheld frother. Start here, then adjust the strength to your taste.

  1. Sift about 1 teaspoon of matcha into your bowl.
  2. Add a small splash of warm water and mix into a smooth paste.
  3. Add more warm water and whisk briskly until mixed and lightly foamy.
  4. Enjoy it straight, or pour over milk and ice for a latte.

A LITTLE EXTRA HELP

A smoother cup.

Clumps happen. A few small adjustments can make preparation easier.

  • Sift first to break up dry clumps.
  • Use warm water rather than freshly boiling water.
  • Whisk in a quick W-shaped motion.
  • Keep your matcha tightly sealed, away from heat and light.
