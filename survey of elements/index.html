Distillery Tours

Nestled off the west coast of Scotland, the island of Islay is renowned for its distinct peat-smoked malts and time-honored distillation traditions. Here, ten iconic distilleries each offer a unique exploration into their craft, where you can witness the art and science behind each bottle, savor exclusive tastings, and immerse yourself in the storied landscapes that define Islay's spirited essence. Whether you're a seasoned aficionado or a curious newcomer, our comprehensive table of Islay's distilleries will guide you on an unforgettable journey through the flavors and tales of this enchanting island.

Finlaggan

Finlaggan is a historic site with immense cultural and historical significance. It was once the epicenter of the Lordship of the Isles, a powerful kingdom that ruled over the Hebrides and parts of western Scotland from the 12th to the late 15th century. The site comprises several small islands on Loch Finlaggan, the most notable being Eilean Mor and Eilean na Comhairle, which were centers of political and ceremonial activities. Here, the Island Council, comprising clan leaders and the Lords of the Isles, would meet to govern their territories. Today, visitors can explore the ruins of ancient buildings and gain a deeper understanding of medieval Scottish history through the interpretive center and informative displays.

Quick facts:

  • Once the seat of the Lordship of the Isles
  • Important political center from 12th to 15th century
  • Located on two main islands, Eilean Mor and Eilean na Comhairle
  • Visitors can explore historical ruins and an interpretive center
  • Offers insight into the medieval clans' governance and culture

The Corryvreckan

The Corryvreckan is one of the most famous and formidable natural whirlpools in the world, located between the islands of Jura and Scarba off the west coast of Scotland. Known for its turbulent and unpredictable waters, the Corryvreckan is caused by strong Atlantic currents meeting deep basins and the unique underwater topography of the area, which includes a pinnacle that rises sharply from the seabed. This combination of factors generates powerful tidal forces and roaring whirlpools, particularly during the spring tides, attracting adventurous boaters and thrill-seekers eager to witness its impressive swells and surges.
